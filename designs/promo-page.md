## Problem Statement

PROMO.md is accurate, but it reads like a README. It has no single moment that a course/demo audience will remember. Its sample is also broken: the `Try it` command uses `--last-period 2025-03-01`, which the tool now rejects ("621 days ago, more than 7 days past your 28-day cycle"). The sample output is also edited. It drops the `Not nutrition advice` line, the `Nutrients that matter now` header and the `note: chosen over oranges` line.

## What Makes This Cool

Most cycle-nutrition apps confidently promise the right foods for your phase. This tool grades its own advice. On day 3 it gives real USDA data with Strong and Moderate evidence. On day 14 it gives placeholder foods and labels its own pairings Speculative and Weak. When the same tool shows both days side by side, it argues against its own hype. That's the moment the audience should remember.

## Constraints

- Audience: the course/demo audience (decided earlier today). Shorter and warmer.
- AGENTS.md privacy: invented dates only, no real cycle data, no usernames or local paths.
- Contrast with other apps: summarize the common promise in your own words. No app names, no quotes (decided by the user).
- Content only: no code changes.

## Premises (agreed)

1. The hook is the self-own: a day-3 vs day-14 side-by-side.
2. Every sample is the tool's real, unedited output, run with invented dates.
3. `Try it` doesn't use a fixed date. It gives the tool's exact date rule (see outline item 8).
4. The common "eat for your phase" promise is described in your own words, with no names or quotes.
5. The deliverable is PROMO.md content. Distribution is the repo itself.

## Landscape (generic search, 2026-11-12)

- The usual pitch: phase-matched recipes, meal plans and "hormone balance" personalization, stated with confidence.
- The evidence: news coverage (Dec 2023) of a McMaster University review reported little evidence for adapting diet and exercise to cycle phase. The exact paper, journal and scope weren't verified this session, so it is background only and stays off the page.
- This matches the project's own EVIDENCE.md: 2 Strong, 4 Moderate, 1 Weak, 5 Speculative out of 12.

## Approaches Considered

- A) Minimal patch: keep the sections, swap the sample, fix Try it. Rejected because it leaves the hook buried.
- B) Rebuild around the self-own. **Chosen.**
- C) Demo-script page ("Meet day 3 / Meet day 14"). Not chosen because it's less scannable for someone reading the repo alone.

## Recommended Approach: B

Page outline, in order:

1. **Headline:** a cycle food planner that tells you when it's guessing.
2. **The usual promise (2-3 sentences, your words):** apps say they know the right foods for each phase. The page's counter-claim rests only on the project's own EVIDENCE.md review: of 12 claims, 5 are Speculative and 1 is Weak. No outside study is cited on the page.
3. **Day 3 vs day 14:** two real output blocks, unedited. Neither block shows a `$ python3 plan.py ...` line or any date. The captions use relative wording only: "a period that started 2 days ago" and "a period that started 13 days ago", both with a 28-day cycle. That way nothing on the page holds a date that can go stale. Day 3 shows the real FDC data with Strong/Moderate evidence. Day 14 shows `[placeholder]` foods with Speculative/Weak evidence, and its caption says in plain words that the placeholder foods are unfinished.
   - **Season caveat:** plan.py picks foods by the season of the run date. In autumn, day 3 shows broccoli with `note: chosen over oranges - out of season`. From 1 December (winter), oranges wins and the note line disappears. Day 14's pears also change to oats. So the captions must not promise the note line or any specific food. If the note is shown, it's described as "for example, in autumn".
   - **Clean runs:** every sample and check run happens in a fresh scratch folder that holds only plan.py and nutrition_rules.json. Never copy history.json from the working checkout, and never run in the checkout itself, so the user's real history.json and nutrients.md are never read or overwritten. The folder is recreated before each regeneration, because stored history (no repeats within 2 cycles) changes the picks.
4. **The scoreboard:** 12 phase-nutrient claims checked: 2 Strong, 4 Moderate, 1 Weak, 5 Speculative. Link to EVIDENCE.md.
5. **What I chose to build:** keep the current bullets (evidence next to every pick, real data, open about its choices, private by design), shortened.
6. **What it isn't:** keep the current caveats (not medical advice, only Menstrual has real data, and the good evidence is for daily supplements, not phase-timed food).
7. **How I built it:** keep the current paragraph (OpenSpec, PLANNING_LOG.md, PROCESS.md).
8. **Try it:** labelled as a template: `python3 plan.py --last-period YYYY-MM-DD --cycle-length DAYS`, with 28 given as an example and a note that the tool accepts 21-38 days. The instruction matches the tool's rule: "the start date of your most recent period, not in the future, and no more than your cycle length plus 7 days ago". Mention `--exclude CATEGORY` with `--exclude fish` shown as a bare flag to add to the template, not as a full command. Don't show a fixed `--prefer-alternative` example, because the nutrient must be in the reader's current phase. Instead, say that when the output has a `swap with --prefer-alternative ...` note, you can copy that flag, and put quotes around a two-word nutrient, for example `--prefer-alternative "vitamin c"` (the note prints it without quotes). This is also a bare flag. Add one line saying the tool remembers recent picks in local files (history.json, nutrients.md) in the folder it runs in, so foods rotate between cycles. That also supports "private by design".

## Success Criteria

- The page has no runnable full commands, only the Try it template and bare flags. In a fresh scratch folder, the template runs without error with a valid date filled in, both on its own and with each bare flag added (`--exclude fish` with any valid date, `--prefer-alternative "vitamin c"` with a Menstrual-phase date).
- On demo day, re-running both samples in a fresh scratch folder (period start 2 and 13 days before demo day, 28-day cycle) gives exactly the blocks on the page. If the season has changed the output, the blocks are updated first.
- A reader who sees only the first screen can say what makes this tool different.
- No app names, no quotes from other apps, no real personal data.
